I connect payment providers and find out why payments hang: webhooks, retries, idempotency, reconciliation. Python and Django, Celery, Kafka, PostgreSQL — on production systems where a bug costs money.

Paid at the provider, pending on your side?

A payment that sits in pending for hours is almost never stuck at the provider. The money moved, or didn't, within seconds. What is stuck is your knowledge of it: the callback carrying the final status was never applied. Between the provider and the row in your database there are seven places where that happens, and each one has its own check.

The map, with the incidents behind it: Seven places a webhook dies between the provider and your database.

How an audit starts

  1. You describe the symptom and send the id of one stuck payment. I reply with which of the seven places it usually is, and what I would need to read.
  2. You export what you can: payments and their status history, inbound webhook logs, delivery attempts. callback-audit runs the first pass on those files, on your side if you prefer.
  3. Within a week: the document, and a walk-through of it.

Real bugs in widely-used payment integrations, reproduced against their own code and reported upstream:

  • WooCommerce Stripe Gateway: a webhook that fails signature validation is answered 204, so Stripe never retries and the order stays pending. Case · upstream
  • Mollie Payments for WooCommerce: when the status request to Mollie fails, the webhook still answers 200, so Mollie stops retrying. Case · upstream (confirmed by Mollie)
  • Razorpay for WooCommerce: with the Payment Action set to "Authorize", the order of an authorized payment is marked Failed, and the cron marks events done even when the payment couldn't be fetched. Case · upstream
  • Odoo with Mollie: a failed status request is acknowledged with 200, so the transaction stays in draft. Case · upstream
